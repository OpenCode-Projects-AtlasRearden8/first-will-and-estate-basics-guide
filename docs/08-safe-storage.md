# Safe Storage: So Your Papers Can Actually Be Found

A perfect plan in an unknown drawer equals no plan. Storage isn't an afterthought
to the documents — it's the last mile that makes the documents real. The design
goal is embarrassingly simple:

> **Any two adults you trust can find the right paper within a day, without asking you.**

## The two-layer system

Practically every working setup uses both layers:

**Physical folder — "The Green Folder"** (call yours whatever; people remember a name)
- One clearly-labeled physical folder or archive box, in one fixed location.
- Contains originals or copies: will, POAs, directives, letter of instruction,
  storage map, deed/titles/policies receipts, pet records.
- Not a fireproof safe that requires your face to open — a folder that a trusted
  person with a key can reach. (Fire- and water-resistance are real concerns; use
  a modest fire-rated document bag or box *inside* an accessible location.)

**Digital vault — "The Index"**
- A short document (1 page) listing each item, its physical location, and pointers
  to digital copies.
- Encrypted digital copies: a password-protected archive or scanned docs stored in
  a location your people are *authorized* to access (a shared encrypted drive or a
  service with documented legacy/delegate access — the alternative is an unopenable
  vault).
- Password manager emergency-access or legacy-contact feature clearly described in
  the letter of instruction — *pointer only, never the secrets themselves*.

## Locations, ranked for findability

| Location | Findability | Fire/flood | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Labeled folder in a shared closet/drawer at home | Excellent | Weak (mitigate with a doc bag) | Choose somewhere a helper would logically look; tell them exactly where |
| With your attorney (original will) | Very good | Generally very good | Only helps if family knows the attorney's name — put it in the letter |
| Safe-deposit box | Good | Excellent | Access needs proof + signer status; **do not** store the *only* copy of POAs or directives there — hospitals don't keep bank hours. If your state seals boxes at death, account for the delay |
| Home fireproof safe | Good | Good | **Key/combination must be findable too** — a safe nobody can open is a paperweight |
| Only on your computer/phone | Poor | N/A | Locked device = locked plan; never the only copy |

## Redundancy rules of thumb

- **One copy:** unacceptable for anything your family might need urgently
  (directives, POAs, the letter). Give real copies to your people.
- **Two copies:** the floor for originals-style documents (attorney + folder, etc.).
- **Pointers everywhere, secrets nowhere:** every location list says *where* the
  master of each thing is. Passwords and account numbers stay in your password
  manager / sealed envelope, referenced by the letter, never written into shared docs.
- **Version labels:** date every document, every copy. "Will — signed 2026-03-14"
  prevents the classic two-competing-originals nightmare.

## Telling people (the step everyone skips)

Storage only works if at least one or two people have rehearsed it. The good news:
this is a 10-minute conversation.

- *"If anything ever happened to me, the green folder in the hall closet is the
  whole map."*
- Walk through it once: open it, show the folder, show the checklist, name the
  people. Done.
- Same for the remote version: *"It's also scanned in our shared [approved place] —
  here's how you'd get in."*

Agents under POAs and healthcare proxies **especially** need to know where papers
are before a crisis. Chapter 4's tip about pre-filing POAs with your bank covers
the institutional half; this covers the family half.

## If you travel or your household is digital-first

- Traveling agents: consider a digital copy of directives with the agent directly.
- Households that live in the cloud: the physical green folder can be small —
  what it must contain is the *index* and the *letters* (who to call). Legal
  originals mostly stay with the attorney or bank; your family's need is the map.
- Rehearse once a year with the same energy as a smoke-detector battery: re-date
  the letter, confirm people's numbers, confirm the shared-access still works.
