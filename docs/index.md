# Start Here

Welcome. This guide exists because a phrase like **"estate planning"** is enough to
make most people close the tab — it sounds expensive, lawyer-y, and vaguely funeral-shaped.

It isn't. Estate planning is just **deciding, on a good day, things that are easier to
decide now than later** — and putting those decisions on paper so the people you love
aren't left guessing.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

## The one-paragraph version

You (eventually) want five things:

1. **A will** — says who gets your stuff and who shepherds it there.
2. **Beneficiary designations** — the forms on your accounts that quietly override your will.
3. **Powers of attorney** — who can act for you financially and legally if you're alive but can't.
4. **Healthcare directives** — who can speak medically for you, and what you'd want said.
5. **A family letter of instruction** — the friendly, non-legal companion note with
   practical details (where papers are, who to call, the dog's food brand).

Plus one wrapper: **safe storage** — the answers mean nothing if nobody can find them.

## How to use this guide (small doses welcome)

- Each chapter is self-contained. Read one, take a walk, read another. This is an
  ADHD-friendly manual on purpose: short sections, checklists, no guilt.
- Every chapter ends with a **"Your move"** box — one or two small concrete actions.
- You never need to act on all of it at once. Chapter 7's checklist is built for
  a "one item per weekend" pace.
- The printable, click-off version lives in the HTML site's checklists section
  (`site/index.html`).

## Ground rules

- **This is education, never legal advice.** We explain vocabulary and concepts so
  your conversation with a licensed professional — an estate-planning attorney,
  typically — is faster and cheaper. Rules vary a lot by state and country, and a
  professional is the only one who can tell you what's right for *you*.
- **No gloom.** Planning is done at a calm distance, the way you buy an umbrella —
  not because you want rain, but because you'd hate to be caught in it unprepared.
- **No data left this repository.** Nothing here asks for your private information.
  Workbooks you fill in live wherever you keep them, not inside a repo, and never
  in version control.
