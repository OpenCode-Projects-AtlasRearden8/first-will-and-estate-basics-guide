# Glossary: Plain-English Definitions

Words the estate world uses, explained the way a friend would.

**Advance directive** — Umbrella term for documents that speak for your medical care
when you can't: typically includes a healthcare proxy and a living will.

**Agent (attorney-in-fact)** — The person you authorize to act for you in a power of
attorney. Despite the name, an agent does *not* need to be an attorney.

**Beneficiary** — Anyone named to receive money or property. On accounts, the term
means the person named on the form that outranks the will.

**Beneficiary designation** — That form. Filed with the account or policy holder;
controls where that asset goes, bypassing your will.

**Codicil** — A short, formal amendment to a will. (Modern practice often just
re-executes an updated will instead.)

**Conservatorship / guardianship (of an adult)** — The court process that gives
someone legal authority to act for an adult who can't manage. What a POA is designed
to make unnecessary. Slow, public, expensive — worth avoiding via paperwork.

**Contingent beneficiary** — Your backup beneficiary, used if a primary is unable
to receive. Leave it blank at your peril.

**Durable (POA)** — Survives your incapacitation. The useful kind.

**Executor (personal representative)** — The person your will empowers to gather
your assets, pay valid debts, and distribute per the will, under court supervision.

**Estate** — Everything you own at death, after debts. It turns out this word
includes your frying pan.

**Fiduciary** — Someone legally required to act in *your* best interest. A standard
worth asking for when choosing financial professionals.

**Healthcare proxy / healthcare agent** — The person you appoint to make medical
decisions when you can't communicate.

**Intestate** — Dying without a valid will. State law then distributes property by
default formula. Not fatal to your family's wellbeing, but rarely your preferences.

**Letter of instruction** — Your informal companion document: contacts, locations,
household logistics, wishes. No court filing; enormous practical value.

**Living will** — Written instructions about life-sustaining treatment and end-of-life
care, used when you can't communicate.

**Per stirpes** — "By branch." If a beneficiary dies before you, their share passes
to *their* descendants. (Alternative distribution options exist; read the fine
print or ask the professional.)

**POA (power of attorney)** — A document authorizing someone to act on your behalf.
Financial POAs cover money/legal matters; medical POAs cover healthcare.

**Payable-on-death (POD) / Transfer-on-death (TOD)** — Designations that move bank
accounts (POD) or securities/vehicles/deeds where your state allows (TOD) to a
named beneficiary outside of probate.

**Personal representative** — See executor. Some states officially use this term.

**Probate** — The court-supervised process of validating a will and settling an
estate. Duration and cost vary widely by state and estate. Whether/how to streamline
it is a professional conversation.

**Self-proving affidavit** — A sworn statement (usually notarized) attached to a
will, signed by you and witnesses, that helps the court accept the will without
tracking down witnesses later. Availability varies by state.

**Springing POA** — Takes effect only upon a defined triggering event (typically a
doctor's certification of incapacity), in states that allow them.

**Testator** — The person making a will. You, on signing day.

**Trust** — A legal container: a **grantor** puts assets under a **trustee's**
management for **beneficiaries**, per the trust's written rules. Trusts can avoid
probate, stage inheritances over time, protect beneficiaries, or all three —
design territory for professionals.

**Will (last will and testament)** — The core legal document directing distribution
of probate assets and naming your executor (and guardian, if you have minors).

---

*This glossary is education, not legal advice. Definitions simplify; your state's
statutes and a licensed professional supply precision where it matters.*
